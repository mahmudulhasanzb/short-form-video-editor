# Visual Direction: Reusable Style

## PHASE 3 OVERRIDE, 2026-10-30, STYLE IS OPTIONAL, NOT MANDATORY
Read this override first. Where it conflicts with the older rules below, this override wins for new videos.
- The style below (warm grid, rounded glass, blue script highlight, soft shadow, thin grid, three layouts, two caption modes) is ONE optional direction, not a mandatory default for every video or every scene. A new video may use it, or a different coherent direction, as long as text remains readable and the video stays coherent.
- For each suitable scene, the plan must offer 2 to 3 genuinely different visual approaches, for example typography-led, editorial, data or process diagram, product demonstration, illustration or object, or minimal with no graphic, and the user selects one. Do NOT offer only different cards with the same background and same placement.
- Do NOT force every visual idea into a card. A scene may use typography only, a diagram, an object, an image, or no graphic, with Full Speaker as the fallback.
- Keep the good rules below that do not force repetition: vertical 9:16, speaker first, hook first, tight cuts, no dead air, captions 3 to 4 words by default with Apple animation, dark text on light canvas and white only on dark speaker background, topic-timed UI, no blank split, no invented stats, and full-face split crop measured per video.
- Do NOT add decorative elements only to make a scene look complex. If an element does not help the viewer understand the spoken idea, remove it.
- Font note, corrected: Creato Display is MISSING from assets/fonts/. The current Primary Sans stopgap is Nexa, ExtraLight mapped to 400 and Heavy mapped to 700, with a weight compromise, and Instrument Serif is secondary. Do NOT claim Creato Display is the rendered primary font until a real Creato Display file is present in assets/fonts/. Normal and highlighted fonts are independently selectable in the plan, with live preview.

## Scope

Style source for this document: two provided reference videos, represented by 20 frames only.

- Reference video 1: 49.75 seconds, 1080x1920, 30fps.
- Reference video 2: 35.24 seconds, 720x1280, 30fps.
- Frames kept: 10 frames per video, maximum, only frames where a new editing style appears first. No repeated frames.
- Frames location: in the Visual Training Storyboard, and in a frames folder next to it, as reference-video-1 (10 frames) and reference-video-2 (10 frames).
- Full videos are not included in any project folder for agent use. Work from this document plus the 20 frames. Do not re-analyse full videos for style.

This is a reusable style for new videos. Use the rules, layouts, caption modes, and card types. Do not copy the reference videos' specific words, numbers, products, or graphics into a new video. New videos use their own script, their own numbers, and their own products, in the same style.

Format for every video: vertical 9:16. No intro logo. No dead air. No pauses, filler words, or repeated takes in the final cut. Graphic changes happen on a key word.

# 1. Talking head and hook

## Talking Head Breakdown
- **Main Speaker**: Show the main speaker first, centred, head-and-shoulders, with a dark background and a visible microphone.
- **Starting**: Speaker first, then the idea immediately. No logo, no title card, no slow intro.
- **Background Treatment**: In Full Speaker scenes, the background behind captions and UI is blurred and darkened. The graphic canvas is not darkened in the same way, it uses the warm off-white grid background in Section 4.
- **Cutting**: Tight, word level cuts. If a word, pause, or take does not move the idea forward, cut it.

## Hook Editing

### 1. Shows Main Speaker
- There is always a shot of the speaker first, so the viewer can decide if they want to watch this person or not.

### 2. One Problem, Then Structure Preview
- The hook states one clear problem in the first seconds.
- Then it previews the structure of the video with a UI element, for example numbered circles that fill in as chapters are covered, or a slider from beginner to professional.
- Then the chapters follow in that same order. Do not preview one order and deliver another.

### 3. Hook Messaging
- The editor thinks: what is the main thing that will make the viewer want to watch this video in the hook, and visualizes it.
- Highlight only that key idea or number, not every word. The highlight is the large script word in Section 2, not a full sentence in script font.
- Specific hook words always come from the new video's own script. Never copy a hook word, number, or claim from a reference video into a new video.

- No specific folder assigned for talking head alone. Talking head examples are in the 20 storyboard frames.

# 2. Captions and typography

## How we create Captions on Our Videos

### Fonts
- We always use a simple white sans font for the regular text and we make sure this simple modern sans font has many font types [INFERRED, NOT VERBATIM: (e.g.] normal medium and black and thin) so that we can vary them in the edit.
- We also use a script font for special words and we make sure this script font is always the opposite of the sans font (if the sans [INFERRED, NOT VERBATIM: is thin,] the script is bold and vice versa).

#### Font Types and Locked Font Selection
- **Font Types to Note**: Sans, Script, and Serif.
- **Primary Font**: `Creato Display` (Sans), available in the project `fonts` folder.
- **Secondary Font**: `Instrument Serif` (Serif), from the `fonts` folder, locked for this style.
- **Additional Reference Fonts (Unassigned)**: Listed from `fonts` folder for reference only:
  - `bryson`
  - `built_titling`
  - `heading_now`
  - `nexa`
  - `playfair_display`
- Unassigned fonts are not used in the locked style unless a scene plan specifically calls for them. Do not mix them in randomly.

### Colors
- **Sans Text Color**: Typically we use a simple white for the modern sans text (`#F3F3F3`), not white all the way, just slightly warm.
- **Script Text Color**: Bright blue, large, italic, with a premium gradient within the same blue colour family, plus a soft glow in the same blue. See Premium Gradient below.
- No hex code in this document is invented. If a colour is marked to confirm, it stays unconfirmed until it is read from a storyboard frame, not guessed.

### Premium Gradient for Highlighted Words
- Highlighted key words do not use a flat solid colour with a hard outline, and do not use a white glow, and do not use a multi-colour gradient.
- Use a premium gradient within the same colour only: for blue key words, a very light gradient from a lighter blue to a deeper blue, top to bottom, with a soft glow in the same blue at low opacity.
- The gradient difference stays very light. The word must stay readable at small vertical sizes, and the glow must not blur the letter shapes.
- Normal sans words stay flat in `#F3F3F3`, with a light drop shadow only. The gradient is for highlighted script words only.

### Caption Modes, Two Modes Only

#### Mode A: Talking Caption
- Use this in Full Speaker scenes, and in the graphic layer of 50/50 Split scenes. See Section 3 for split layer rules.
- Small sans for normal words, much larger blue script italic for key words only.
- No long subtitle paragraphs. The caption follows the spoken words, word by word, but only key words get the large script treatment.
- Placement in Full Speaker: lower thirds, centre aligned, over the speaker's chest area, never over the face or eyes.
- Placement in 50/50 Split: inside the top graphic layer only, at the bottom of that layer, above the person layer, never on the person layer.
- Scale: keep normal text smaller than normal, since it makes the design look higher quality, and we scale [INFERRED, NOT VERBATIM: them up] and make it bigger to drag more attention to it, for key words only.

#### Mode B: Statement Lockup
- Use this only in 100% Full Canvas scenes. This is not a subtitle, and it is not a full transcription.
- A curated headline statement, small sans plus large script, placed top or centre, with one graphic in the centre and generous empty space around it.
- Use this for a chapter title, a key claim, or a recap line, not as a running caption.
- Do not use Mode B and Mode A at the same time on the same frame. Choose one mode per scene, based on layout.

### Placement, General Rule
- Placement is decided by layout, not by habit. Full Speaker: lower thirds. 50/50 Split: top graphic layer plus clean person layer. 100% Full Canvas: statement top or centre.
- Never place a bottom subtitle bar over a Full Canvas graphic. Never cover the main graphic, the badge, or the key number with a caption. Never cover a face or eyes with a caption, card, or badge.

### Effects
- We use a glow effect to highlight special words in the script font, and we use a light drop shadow on the norm[INFERRED, NOT VERBATIM: al text to] make them stand out from the background.
- Glow on special script words, in the same colour as the word, light drop shadow on normal text.

### Animation
- All talking captions follow a word by word pop up animation, the position of each word pops up from slightly under the [INFERRED, NOT VERBATIM: baseline and] also fades in, and as it fades in it also has a very light blur in animation, the trick is to smoothly animate the te[INFERRED, NOT VERBATIM: xt back] down, all together the duration of the animation of the text is never longer than 1.5s.
- Word by word pop up, each word rising slightly from below, fading in, with a very light blur. Total animation never longer than 1.5 seconds.
- Pop animations are short. A pop is not a slow fade, and it is not a slide across the screen.

# 3. Scene types for B-roll and animations

## Layout Modes, Apply to Every Scene

Every scene plan must state both a content type from the 8 types below, and a layout type from this list. Content type alone is not enough, because the same content looks wrong in the wrong layout.

### Layout 1: Full Speaker
- Speaker fills the frame.
- Caption in Mode A, lower thirds, over the chest area, never over the face or eyes.
- UI card, if any, below the caption in dark glass, over the chest area, never over the face.
- Background darkened and blurred behind the card.
- Use for: hook, talking explanation, recap, CTA.

### Layout 2: 50/50 Split, Two Separate Layers
- This layout is built as two separate layers, not as one flat image with text pasted on top of the person.
- **Layer A, top, graphic layer, 0 to 50 percent of final frame height**: warm off-white, thin grey grid, graphic in the centre. All captions, UI cards, badges, arrows, and labels for this scene live in this layer only.
- **Layer B, bottom, person layer, 50 to 100 percent of final frame height**: person only, clean. Nothing is placed on this layer. No caption, no card, no badge, no arrow, no graphic, no text of any kind on the person layer.
- **Caption in split scenes**: caption text is part of Layer A, placed at the bottom of Layer A, on the graphic background, just above the person's head. It is not an overlay on Layer B. In the reference frames, a caption word sits in the gap between the graphic and the head, on the light background, not over the face.
- **Split line**: clean horizontal split at 50 percent. No heavy divider. Graphic background ends, person background begins.
- Use for: explaining a graphic while the speaker stays visible, showing a card, a graph, or a mockup next to the speaker.

#### Rule: Never leave a 50/50 Split screen blank.
Layout is per beat, not fixed per scene. Use 50/50 Split only for a beat that has a relevant UI card, a sourceReference file, or other relevant visual in the top canvas. Captions alone do not justify a split.
If a beat has no relevant card or source, return to Full Speaker for that beat.
When returning to Full Speaker, use Full Speaker styling: captions white (#FFFFFF) on the dark background, normal 52px, highlighted 84px, weight 600, full face and shoulders visible.
Switch only at a chunk boundary, never mid-word, with no blank frame in between. Use the existing card exit (0.30s) before the switch.
Full Canvas scenes are not affected, because the Mode B headline is the visual there.

#### Eye Line Crop Rule for the Person Layer, Read This Before Building a Split
- Do not build a split by taking the bottom 50 percent of the original talking head video. That cut would cut through the head and eyes.
- Build the person layer by cropping the original talking head video to a person strip first, then placing that strip in the bottom 50 percent. The crop is set from the eye line, not from a fixed pixel value.
- **Step 1, measure the original frame**: on a clean talking frame, mark three lines: head top, eye line, chin, and shoulders. In the reference frames, a full speaker frame has head top at about 17 percent from the top, eye line at about 26 percent from the top, and chin at about 39 percent from the top. Your own recording will differ, so measure your own frame, do not copy these percentages blindly.
- **Step 2, top cut**: cut from the top down to just above the head, leaving only small headroom. Headroom is about 5 percent of the person strip height above the head top. In the reference framing, this meant cutting about 12 to 18 percent of the original frame height from the top, the empty background above the head, not cutting into hair.
- **Step 3, bottom cut**: cut from the bottom up to just below the shoulders and microphone, keeping shoulders and microphone visible, removing hands, lower torso, and empty lower area. In the reference framing, this meant cutting about 35 to 45 percent of the original frame height from the bottom, leaving a person strip about 40 to 50 percent of the original frame height.
- **Step 4, place the strip**: place the person strip in Layer B, bottom 50 percent of the final 9:16 frame, centred horizontally, with no distortion, no stretching, and no zoom that changes face proportions.
- **Step 5, check the final eye position**: after placement, eyes must land at about 62 to 65 percent down the final frame. That is: speaker layer starts at 50 percent, plus eyes at about 25 to 30 percent down the person strip, which is 12.5 to 15 percent of final frame height, giving 62.5 to 65 percent total. In the reference split frames, eyes land at about 62 to 68 percent down the final frame, depending on the scene.
- **Step 6, check head and chin in the strip**: head top must be 2 to 5 percent below the top edge of Layer B, never touching the split line and never cropped at the top. Chin must be fully visible, at about 45 to 55 percent down Layer B. Shoulders must fill the bottom of Layer B. Microphone may reach the bottom edge, face must not be cut.
- **Step 7, if the speaker moves**: if the speaker leans, nods, or changes distance, re-check the eye line for that scene. Keep eyes in the 62 to 65 percent band in the final frame by adjusting the crop for that scene, not by randomly scaling the face larger or smaller, and not by placing text over the face to hide a bad crop.
- If a crop cuts hair, cuts chin, or puts eyes outside the 62 to 68 percent band, the crop is wrong. Fix the top and bottom cuts, do not fix it by adding text or a card over the person.

### Layout 3: 100% Full Canvas
- No speaker on screen.
- Warm off-white grid canvas, full frame.
- Statement lockup in Mode B, top or centre, one graphic in the centre, generous empty space.
- Use for: chapter titles, key statements, a single graphic that needs full attention, a graph, or a product card sequence.

## 4 Families and 8 Scene Types

### 1. Text Centric Animations
- **Text Only**:
  - *Scenes with only text*: these scenes place the text in the middle and that's it. Typical layout: 100% Full Canvas, Mode B.
- **Text + Object**:
  - *Scenes with text and an object*: These scenes allow the text to take up the majority of the screen and leave an object in secondary view to add more depth. Typical layout: 100% Full Canvas, or 50/50 Split.
- **Text + Graphic**:
  - *Scenes with text and graphic shapes*: these scenes place the text in the middle and support it with graphic shapes around it. Typical layout: 100% Full Canvas, or 50/50 Split.
- **Inspiration Grid**:
  - `inspiration/text centered scenes/`

### 2. Person Centric Scenes
- **Person in center**:
  - *Scenes highlighting a person as the focus*: these scenes place people and typically place a graphic behind them in order to support them and emphasize the person on the screen. Typical layout: Full Speaker.
- **Person on Side**:
  - *Scenes placing a person with text side by side*: these scenes allow the people to take the majority of the screen while still showing our text and who they are. Typical layout: 50/50 Split, person in the bottom layer, text and graphic in the top layer only.
- **Inspiration Grid**:
  - No specific folder assigned.

### 3. Object Centric Animations
- **Object (Middle) + Text**:
  - *Scenes with objects in the center and text on top or under*: these scenes place the main object in the middle and surround it with text that supports the idea either on top of the object or below. Typical layout: 100% Full Canvas, or 50/50 Split, with text in the graphic layer only.
- **Object + Graphic**:
  - *Scenes with objects and graphics around*: these scenes place the main object in the middle and create graphics either behind or around the object in order to emphasize it. Typical layout: 100% Full Canvas, or 50/50 Split.
- **Inspiration Grid**:
  - No specific folder assigned.

### 4. Graphic Centric Animation
- **Graphic Centric Animation**:
  - *Scenes which focus on a graphic in the middle to deliver the point*: these scenes place a graphic in the middle in order to deliver a point and usually combine it with an object or text. Typical layout: 100% Full Canvas, or 50/50 Split.
- **Inspiration Grid**:
  - `inspiration/Illustration inspirations/`

### 5. Documentary 3D Inspiration Grid (Separate)
- **Documentary 3D**:
  - Kept as its own distinct inspiration category, not merged into Object Centric or Graphic Centric.
- **Inspiration Grid**:
  - `inspiration/documentary 3d/`

## B-roll and Additional Footage, Reference Rule
- B-roll and additional footage never appear as full-screen stock.
- They appear inside the graphic canvas only, as one of these: tilted photo card, browser window mockup, timeline card, sound-wave card, or product or logo card.
- Cards have rounded corners and soft shadow. One idea per card. Do not stack three unrelated clips in one scene.
- If a clip does not fit inside a card in the graphic canvas, do not use it as a full-screen cutaway in this style.

# 4. Minimal animation style principles

## Graphic Canvas Backgrounds (3 Variants)
1. **Warm Off-White + Grid + Cast Shadow**: Warm off-white, thin grey grid, cast shadow overlay. This is the main graphic canvas in the reference videos.
2. **Plain White**: A plain white solid, for simple statement scenes only.
3. **White + Cast Shadow + Grid**: A white and greyish gradient combined with a cast shadow overlay and a thin light grid.

## The Principles of the Minimal Animation Style (4 Steps in Order)

### 1 - Background
- Generally warm off-white or white (warm or cold, as above).
- Light thin grid (grey).
- Cast shadow overlay (light).

### 2 - Focal Point
- Assess the scene's main focus and place it in the main focal zone (center of a 3 by 3 grid).
- **Rules**:
  - Object is the main focus, object is in main zone.
  - Text is the main focus, text is in main zone.
  - Person is the main focus, person is in main zone.

### 3 - Add Supporting Element
- Add supporting elements around main object to emphasize it.
- **Rules**:
  - Object is the main focus, object is in main zone plus text on top for emphasis.
  - Text is the main focus, text is in main zone plus object on the right for emphasis.

### 4 - Add Depth Elements
- Add elements outside the focal point to create depth (outer regions of 3 by 3 grid).
- **Rules**:
  - Object is the main focus, object is in main zone plus objects and graphics outside for depth.
  - Text is the main focus, text is in main zone plus object and graphic outside for depth.
- Stop at one main idea per scene. Adding more depth elements does not make a scene better, it makes it crowded.

## Motion, Zoom, Fade, and Blur
- **Pop in**: Cards, text, and UI elements pop in, with a short pop, not a slow fade.
- **Snap scale for graphics**: A graphic can snap from one scale to another, for example 100 percent to 75 percent, when it needs to make room for a badge, a caption, or a second card. The snap is quick and deliberate.
- **Slow push-in on speaker**: Slow, steady push-in on the talking head, never a fast jump cut zoom.
- **Slow zoom out, then snap on graphics**: Graphics can zoom out slowly, then snap to the next scale or layout. Do not combine a slow zoom and a snap in the same beat without a reason.
- **Camera tracks text writing**: When text writes out letter by letter, the camera or focus follows the writing, it does not stay static over a finished block.
- **Fade and blur, limited use only**: Blur is background darkening and blurring behind UI in Full Speaker, plus very light blur on caption entry. No heavy crossfade between scenes. Cuts are clean, graphics change on a key word.
- **Ending motion**: Return to Full Speaker for recap and CTA, with no extra outro animation after the CTA.

# 5. Design principles and on-screen UI

## The Design Principles (5 Rules)

### 1 - Color
- **3-Tone Palette**: Use a 3 tone color palette made up of black, white, and a special color (for example, black + white + green, black + white + red, black + white + purple, as a general palette idea, not as a rule to copy a specific reference palette into every video).
- **60-30-10 Rule**:
  - Ratio: 60% Primary, 30% Secondary, 10% Accent.
  - **Locked Palette for This Style**:
    - **Primary (60%)**: dark navy [HEX TO CONFIRM FROM STORYBOARD FRAMES]
    - **Secondary (30%)**: light blue [HEX TO CONFIRM FROM STORYBOARD FRAMES]
    - **Accent (10%)**: bright blue [HEX TO CONFIRM FROM STORYBOARD FRAMES]
  - No hex code in this document is invented. If a hex is marked to confirm, it stays unconfirmed until it is read from a storyboard frame, not guessed.

### 2 - Fonts
- Use sans fonts for main text and script or other for highlighted special text.
- See Section 2 for the locked fonts. Do not introduce a new font mid-video.

### 3 - Texts
- Use gradients (very light difference) for the main color of the text, and for highlighted script words use the premium gradient within the same colour in Section 2.
- Gradient difference stays very light. It must not turn the text into a rainbow or a high contrast effect.

### 4 - Objects
- Use cast shadows + textures on objects.
- Objects sit on the grid canvas with a soft shadow, not floating with no shadow and not with a hard black drop shadow.

### 5 - Backgrounds
- Use gradients + Textures + Cast Shadows for Backgrounds.
- Backgrounds stay quiet. The background supports the focal point, it does not compete with it.

## On-Screen UI / On-Screen Animation
- **Placement and Scale, Per Layout**:
  - Full Speaker: animation in the lower thirds, taking up big space, whatever the animation may be, over the chest area, never over the face.
  - 50/50 Split: animation in the top graphic layer only. Person layer stays clean.
  - 100% Full Canvas: animation centred, with generous empty space, one idea at a time.
- **Background Treatment**: Darken and blur the background behind the animation to make the lower third animation more visible, in Full Speaker only. In graphic canvas scenes, use the warm off-white grid background, not a dark blur.
- **UI Card Style**: Glossy, rounded, white or dark glass, soft shadow, thin grid background behind it, dashed outline only where a boundary matters, and a small badge only where a number matters.
- **Graphs, Simple Only**: One slider, one loading bar with one segment lit, three colour swatches, one timeline, or one set of simple bars. One idea per card, one graph per scene, unless the scene plan specifically calls for a comparison of two simple graphs.

### Glossy Card Examples, From the Reference Frames Only (Examples, Not Rules)
- These are examples of what a card can look like in a specific video. Do not copy them into an unrelated video just because they worked in a reference video.
- Dark glass pill with numbered circles, for a chapter or step preview.
- Slider from beginner to professional, for a progress or level idea.
- Tilted photo card inside the graphic canvas.
- Browser window mockup inside the graphic canvas.
- Timeline card, for a sequence or a sound timeline.
- Sound-wave card, for audio, voice, or sound design moments.
- Logo or product card, for a tool, app, or product name.
- Loading segment card, with one segment lit.
- Three colour swatches, for a palette comparison.
- Dashed outline card with a small badge, where a number matters, for example a percentage change in that specific video.
- A 3D floating graphic as a centre object, for example an eyeball in a video about vision or seeing, only when the topic fits. Do not use an eyeball, a slider, or swatches as a default decoration in every video.

### Do Not Do, In This Style
- Do not use a bottom subtitle bar on a Full Canvas scene.
- Do not use full-screen stock B-roll.
- Do not use long subtitle paragraphs.
- Do not use a heavy crossfade between scenes.
- Do not copy a reference-specific graphic, like an eyeball or a specific product card, into a video where the topic does not fit.
- Do not add a second main idea to a scene that already has one focal point.
- Do not invent a hex code, a number, a product name, or a caption word to fill a card. If it is unknown, mark it unknown and get it from the actual script or frame.
- Do not place anything on the person layer in a 50/50 Split scene.
- Do not build a split by cutting the bottom 50 percent of a talking head video. Use the eye line crop rule in Section 3.

## How To Use This Document For a New Video
- For each scene in the scene plan, state all of these, in order: content type (one of the 8), layout type (Full Speaker, 50/50 Split, or 100% Full Canvas), main focus, caption mode (Mode A or Mode B), UI card need, person crop check for split scenes (eye line, head top, chin), and sound cue.
- Sound cues follow the sound document, in brief: whoosh on movement and scene change, pop or click on element appearance, riser before a reveal, hit on a key point, music low under voice only. Full sound rules, levels, and file names are in the sound document, not duplicated here.
- Ending: return to Full Speaker, recap the structure in order, then one direct CTA line. No extra outro after the CTA.
