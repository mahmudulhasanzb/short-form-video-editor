# Analyze Video

## Goal
Understand the cleaned video and transcript before making visual decisions. The goal is an edit that feels authored for this specific video, not a sequence of interchangeable UI cards.

## Inputs
- Verified cleaned video.
- Word-level transcript with timestamps.
- Optional user-provided images, screen captures, brand assets, or reference material.
- Optional user-provided B-roll / additional footage, including clips that may need source trimming.

## Process
1. Confirm the cleaned video passed verification.
2. Read the transcript and identify topic changes, key claims, examples, lists, contrasts, demonstrations, and emotional emphasis.
3. Map every proposed scene to exact transcript evidence and frame/time ranges.
4. Inspect the corresponding video moments for speaker framing, eye line, gestures, objects, and usable crop boundaries.
5. For each beat, write down the one thing the viewer should understand or feel.
6. Choose the simplest visual form that communicates that idea: speaker-only, a few words of typography, a comparison, a process diagram, a timeline, a relevant image/object, a genuine product/UI demonstration, or another justified composition.
7. Choose Full Speaker, 50/50 Split, or Full Canvas only after the visual idea is clear. A layout must serve the idea; it is not a visual idea by itself.
8. For each supplied image or footage clip, identify its visible subject/action and compare it with the transcript beats. Recommend placement only where the content is relevant; note the matching transcript evidence and intended screen treatment.
9. For supplied footage, recommend a source-media in/out range when a shorter moment is clearly useful. Keep source-media trim frames separate from the scene's project-timeline frames; if the exact useful range cannot be verified, leave it untrimmed and flag the uncertainty.
10. Record uncertainty and missing source material explicitly. Do not invent facts, metrics, product interfaces, brands, or external assets.

## Anti-template visual direction
- Do not assign a UI card to every scene. Many beats should remain speaker-only or use restrained typography.
- Do not use a UI card just because a beat has a number, noun, or keyword. Use a UI metaphor only when the speaker is actually describing a tool, interface, workflow, or measurable state.
- Avoid repeating the same composition in adjacent beats. Change the visual grammar when the underlying idea changes; keep a composition only when continuity is intentional.
- Alternatives must be conceptually different (for example: comparison vs. process diagram vs. object-led visual), not the same card with different colors, labels, or decorative details.
- Prefer one clear focal point and meaningful negative space over stacks of badges, pills, floating panels, decorative grids, or filler graphics.
- Use only details supported by transcript evidence or supplied assets. If a useful visual would require invented facts or an unavailable asset, choose a simpler truthful treatment.
- Reference storyboards are guidance for craft, motion, spacing, and hierarchy only. Never copy their example text, numbers, products, or card sequence into an unrelated video.

## Output
Produce structured analysis for the planner. Do not generate final HTML as the source of truth. The planner must produce data that conforms to the shared scene-plan contract.
